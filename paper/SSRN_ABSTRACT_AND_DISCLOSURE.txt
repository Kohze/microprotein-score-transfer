We asked whether microprotein sequence scores validated for one experimental target remain useful after the assay, sequence input or decision changes. Using published data, we distinguish a core reporter benchmark from retrospective transfer extensions and exploratory diagnostics. Exact-sequence joins, dependence-controlled evaluation and source-only training define the comparisons. Among 1,072 development constructs, annotation-trained composition resemblance correlated negatively with reporter accumulation (Spearman ρ=-0.292), whereas outcome-trained composition and terminal-aware models reached 0.747 and 0.780. Tests using 1,044 identical peptides across reporter contexts, independent canonical tiles and 1,743 paired sequence changes showed that favorable within-context rankings did not imply reliable transfer. Terminal features could improve within-context prediction while reducing cross-context performance, and a model preferred by global correlation was not always preferred for top-decile selection. Source-population exclusions, matched inputs, training-exposure audits and strong published baselines also changed the eligible comparisons and narrowed apparent improvements. The practical implication is to validate a score against the intended measurement, input and decision, and to test paired effects before using a ranking model to prioritize edits. These results define specific transfer boundaries rather than a universal predictor. Native turnover and peptide-dependent activity remain separate experimental questions.
