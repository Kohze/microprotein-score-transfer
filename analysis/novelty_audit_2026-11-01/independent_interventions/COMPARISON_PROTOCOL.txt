INDEPENDENT C-TERMINAL INTERVENTION TRANSFER - BEFORE PREDICTOR ASSOCIATIONS
1 November 2026

Question
Can a frozen sequence model that ranks native peptide reporters predict the
direction/ranking of a paired C-terminal sequence change in another study?
Does source-WT-only calibration change that result? The biological effects
of readthrough and C-terminal context are already demonstrated by Chu et al.
This is a transport test, not a new mechanism or readthrough discovery.

Design status
Selected after Guo perturbation and Voutsinos native-transfer findings.
Published aggregate Chu results and sequence metadata have been inspected.
No model-versus-Chu-outcome association has been evaluated. All choices are
exploratory research extensions; do not call this a preregistered study.

Source and constructs
Use Supplementary Data 1 (official Table S1, SHA-256
71f17b81c7a2a0965e3216a2c16538c074e32f3dc3bba5a0744ec16bc3a2d652),
retrieved via the official Europe PMC supplemental archive. It contains 3,249
paired constructs. Both WT and RT-derived reporter cargos have 58 residues.
The latter is the retained WT suffix plus RT-part cells, not WT58 followed
by a full extension. For 339 pairs, the retained WT part is empty and RT
alone occupies the entire reporter cargo. There are no simple append pairs.
Do not call the measured cargos full-length proteins or the physical edit
the same fixed 10-residue append intervention as the Guo assay.

Sequence and membership rules
Retain standard 58/58-residue pairs with exact WT-suffix/retained-part
agreement, nonidentical endpoints, consistent reported RT length and a unique
ENSG gene mapping. Map by ENST first, then gene symbol, then unambiguous exact
current canonical C58 sequence; preserve the original identifiers. Six gene
name cells are numeric Excel-date artifacts; transcript/sequence mapping
avoids interpreting these as real gene symbols. Two WT/shared-part sequence
inconsistencies are flagged and excluded, not repaired from outcome values.
Historical/current-transcript terminal matching is reported as a sensitivity,
not required for the main population.

Exclude genes shared with retrieved Guo/annotation source inputs (conservative
union of ENSG and available symbols), and any WT or RT cargo sharing >=60%
Smith-Waterman identity with >=80% coverage of both sequences with those
source sequences. Use BLOSUM62, gap open10/extend1; 40% identity is sensitivity.
For the primary all-model common cohort also exclude PAP-training source
genes and exact 30-mer/PAP or first25-mer/human25 training windows in either
endpoint. This prevents treating pretrained PAP features as having independent
gene/sequence provenance merely because the final Ridge head used nORFs.
Show an additional source-head-only cohort without the PAP-training exclusions,
with provenance limitations explicit; do not choose whichever looks better.

Target dependence groups join gene IDs and identical WT/RT sequences across
rows. They are operational clusters, not exhaustive evolutionary families.
No claim of absence of cross-species or remote homology is made for QCDPred.

Outcomes and quality
Use complete WT and RT PSI values in both biological replicates, requiring
>=60 reads for both endpoints in both replicates as in the source methods.
Compute each replicate's RT-minus-WT PSI and their mean; verify against the
released average WT, RT and delta columns. Preserve any disagreements and
missingness rather than changing membership to improve correlations.
Higher PSI means a more stable reporter. These scores do not supply an
endogenous half-life or a peptide-mediated functional endpoint.

Frozen predictions before association
Compute/save/hash every WT and RT prediction, source fit and calibration
receipt before joining target PSI values. No Chu outcomes select source
orientation, sign, penalty, calibration direction or representation.
Reproduce A/T source heads separately from the original GFP-MP and MP-mCherry
WTs using the existing source-only tuning settings and exact feature definitions.
Both contexts must be reported. GFP-MP is the architecture-matched context
reference, not a source chosen for its observed Chu correlation.

Score comparisons
Primary fitted contrast: T-minus-A delta-rank correlation for GFP-trained
heads on the identical primary common cohort; also report both native-endpoint
rank correlations so native fitting is not confused with intervention success.
Report mCherry-trained heads as a predeclared context sensitivity.
Frozen comparisons: PAP human30 on the last30 residues and on full normalized
58-residue cargo, each decomposed into composition and terminal components;
original QCDPred mean complete17-window score and its normalized-cargo
representation; human25 first25 and normalized-cargo references. Report all.
For every frozen baseline additionally fit an increasing isotonic mapping from
its scores to original Guo GFP-MP WT outcomes only and subtract mapped WT/RT
predictions. Record clipping and flat mapped deltas. Calibration is trained
in another assay, and raw/delta absolute errors across score scales are not
directly comparable. It is not Chu-supervised adaptation.

Reporting and uncertainty
Report all-pair native WT/RT and paired delta Spearman correlations, including
negative/near-zero outcomes. Primary paired intervals use 1,000 operational-
cluster bootstrap resamples conditional on fixed fitted predictions, without
claiming full-retraining uncertainty or multiplicity-adjusted discovery.
Also report replicate-specific results, stricter similarity, historical WT
match sensitivity and RT-length strata <=20, 21-57 and >=58 as descriptive.
Classification on the source authors' |delta PSI|>=1 cutoffs is secondary;
do not select an effect threshold or invert a score based on target results.

Decision gate
An independent delta result that survives the construct/provenance controls
would strengthen the transport benchmark. Failure, clipping or a dependence
on a selected representation must narrow the claims. Even a positive result
does not establish native nORF function, a novel degron mechanism, or priority
over existing published variant/degron models.
